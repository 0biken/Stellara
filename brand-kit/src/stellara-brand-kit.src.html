Brand foundation

Why Stellara exists, what it promises, and how it should feel.

A personal tutor that knows your child.

Stellara teaches in short daily steps, checks what has truly been learned, and shows parents exactly where their child stands.

Mission

Help every child build real understanding, one step at a time, and let their family see it happen.

Brand idea

Learning grows. Every skill a child masters becomes a star, and mastered stars sprout.

Over a term the stars join into the child's own sky garden: a sweet, quiet picture of progress that children are proud of and parents can trust.

Personality

Sweet

Soft colours, rounded shapes, a little smile. Feels like a treat.

Not babyish or sugary-loud.

Calm

Unhurried. Gives children room to think.

Not loud, flashy or rushed.

Warm

Kind, patient and close by, like a favourite auntie.

Never nagging or shaming.

Honest

Shows real progress and names what needs practice, plainly.

No hype, no score guarantees.

Curious

Loves a good question and makes learning feel like discovery.

Not random or distracting.

Where Stellara sits

Between a playful daily-habit app and a credible mastery platform. The sweetness lives in colour, shape and the logo; the structure and the reports stay credible. Internal reference only; we never name other brands in marketing.

Logo: the star sprout

A chubby honey star with a small smile, sprouting two matcha leaves from a strawberry bud. It says what Stellara does: learning grows. A soft cocoa outline gives it a sticker feel, and the five rounded points keep it clear of the four-point sparkle that now signals AI.

Switch Daytime and Starlight in the top bar: the wordmark moves from Strawberry Ink to a soft pink, and the mark stays the same.

Anatomy

  • Honey star: five rounded points, plump inner corners. The reward colour, so the logo and the rewards are one family.
  • Face: two dot eyes, a small smile and pink cheeks. Just enough to feel friendly; it never talks and never becomes a mascot.
  • Sprout: two matcha leaves and a strawberry bud. Growth and mastery, and the three brand colours in one small place.
  • Wordmark: lowercase Nunito ExtraBold, tracked slightly tight. Rounded and sweet without being bubbly.

Auntie Stella

The tutor has a name, a voice and a face from day one. The placeholder avatar (braided bun, round glasses, a honey star earring) now wears the sweet palette and the same soft outline as the logo.

Placeholder avatar v2
"Not quite, my dear. Let's look at it together."

Every Nigerian child knows an Auntie Stella: the patient one who sits with you over homework, tells you the truth kindly, and is quietly proud when you get it. She believes you can do it, so you start to believe it too.

  • Warm, not soft on standards. She praises effort and still asks you to try again.
  • Calm. She never rushes, scolds or nags.
  • Proud of you, out loud. She notices real progress and names it.
  • Respectful. She speaks to parents as equals, never as a salesperson.

Expressions

Four calm expressions. She is never shown disappointed in the child.

Smiling

Welcome, good runs, the report header.

Listening

Empty states, onboarding, waiting.

Thinking

Hints and worked explanations.

Proud

Topic mastered, end of term, the parent note.

Where she appears

Hints

"Auntie Stella says: draw both fractions as pizzas."

After a wrong answer

One kind line and the next small step.

Milestones

A short note when a topic is mastered and its star sprouts.

Parent report

"A note from Auntie Stella" with one thing to celebrate and one thing to practise.

Brief for the illustration, later

Aim for
  • A modern Nigerian woman in her thirties or forties, warm and composed
  • Soft, flat, slightly cartoony illustration in the sweet palette, with the same gentle cocoa outline as the logo
  • A small five-point honey star as her signature detail, such as an earring or brooch
  • Expressions kept subtle: listening, smiling, thinking, proud
Avoid
  • Caricature, exaggerated features or stereotypes
  • A teacher with a cane, ruler or stern look
  • Turning the logo star into a second talking character
  • Showing her disappointed in the child

The placeholder files are in docs/assets/auntie-stella/, generated by docs/tools/avatar.mjs. Test the name and the look with pupils and parents in Yoruba, Hausa and Igbo-speaking homes before the final illustration is commissioned.

Colour

Strawberry, honey and matcha on cream, with warm cocoa for text. Each colour has one job. Every swatch below shows its Daytime value on the left and its Starlight value on the right.

Surfaces, text and states

Right and wrong answers always carry a tick or a word as well as a colour. "Not quite" is a dashed strawberry border, never red.

One job per colour

Strawberry

The one action on a screen, links, focus, selection. Never more than one Strawberry button per screen.

Honey

Stars, Stardust and rewards only, so it keeps its meaning. A fill, never text.

Matcha

Correct answers, mastery and growth: progress bars, sprouts, ticks.

Proportion

Cream 46%
Surface 20%
Milk 12%
Strawberry

Mostly cream and space. The sweet colours are accents: a little goes a long way, and that keeps the whole thing calm.

Daytime and Starlight

Our two themes are named for the sky the product is built on. Daytime is cream and honey. Starlight is a warm cocoa, never black or purple, with soft off-white text. By default the product matches the phone; parents can choose in Settings → How Stellara looks. Children have no switch. The words for all three options are on board 8.

Iconography & illustration

Rounded 1.75 px line icons in the text colour, with at most one small honey detail. Illustrations are where the cartoony sticker style lives.

Construction

  • 24 × 24 grid, 2 px padding, 1.75 px stroke, round caps and joins
  • Stars are always five-point and rounded, matching the logo
  • One honey accent at most; the rest uses the text colour, so icons flip with the theme
  • Icons sit beside a text label for children under 8

The growing sky

Each topic is a star. Shape shows the mastery state, so it reads without colour: dashed, half-filled, filled, and filled with a sprout once mastered. Lit stars join into the child's own sky garden.

Illustration style

Sweet stickers

Rounded shapes, flat fills, a gentle cocoa outline. No gradients, glows or heavy shading.

Children like ours

Nigerian children of different skin tones, hair styles and abilities, at home, in uniform, on the school bus. Never stereotypes.

Plenty of air

One subject per picture, lots of cream around it. In lessons, a picture explains one thing and has alt text.

Motion & 3D

Quick to respond, gentle to settle. Presses answer instantly, rewards take a breath, and nothing bounces, flashes or shakes. The scene below is built live with Babylon.js and follows the theme.

A sweet sky: pink, honey and matcha stars drift slowly and lean toward your pointer. Tap to light a star; lit stars join up.

Micro-interactions

Tap to play.

Answering

The tile presses to 97%. Right: matcha fill, a tick, and a honey star rises in. Not quite: a dashed strawberry border and a kind line.

Days in a row

Each day with 5 or more answers adds a star. A missed day becomes a resting cloud, and the run stays safe.

Topic mastered

A line draws to the star, it fills, then the sprout grows. About 1.2 seconds, once, and it can be skipped.

Curves

TokenValueUse
ease-outcubic-bezier(.23, 1, .32, 1)Anything entering or responding: sheets, stars, feedback. Starts fast, so it feels instant.
ease-in-outcubic-bezier(.77, 0, .175, 1)Something moving across the screen, such as the mastery line.

Never ease-in for interface motion: it delays the moment the child is watching. Entrances start at 95% scale with opacity, never from zero.

Voice & writing

Every word is part of the design. Our rule: sweet in moments, plain in facts. Garden and sky words belong to celebrations and the names of things; consent, money, errors and data use plain words. The full guide is document 16 in the build docs.

Ages 4–7 · Auntie Stella

"Well done! You found it."

Up to 8 words a line, read aloud by default. One idea at a time.

Ages 8–12 · Auntie Stella

"That's four in a row. I see you."

Up to 15 words. Specific about what went right.

Parents · Stellara

"Tolu has mastered Fractions. Next: Decimals."

Plain, warm, specific. Evidence first. No exclamation marks.

Exam names appear in plain text only, never with exam-body logos, with a line saying Stellara is not affiliated with any exam body.

Six rules

Kind and true

Never shame or exaggerate, never hide a problem. "Not quite" is kind and honest.

Specific beats general

"9 of the last 10 right" beats "Great progress!"

One idea per line

Children get one short idea. Parents get the fact first, then the detail.

Same thing, same name

"Start today's set" opens "Today's set". "Send invite" ends in "Invite sent".

Sweet in moments, plain in facts

Sprouts and starlight for celebrations; plain words for consent and errors.

Nothing to lose

Progress only grows. Never lose, break, miss, fail, behind or last chance.

WhatsApp, SMS and email

WhatsApp is the main channel for parents. SMS is the fallback, so it avoids ₦ and emoji, which would cut a message from 160 characters to 70. Email carries the weekly report for email sign-ups, school messages and anything legal.
