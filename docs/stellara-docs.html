1. Vision

A personal tutor that knows your child. Stellara teaches in short daily steps, checks what has truly been learned, and shows parents exactly where their child stands, from kindergarten to the Common Entrance examination.

Learning grows. Every skill a child masters becomes a star, and mastered stars sprout. Over a term the stars join into the child's own sky: something children are proud of and parents can trust.

2. Why now, and for whom

Stellara has direct relationships with a number of private primary schools. Those schools report two problems:

  1. Parents want proof of progress. Termly report cards and occasional WhatsApp messages from teachers are not enough.
  2. Schools want better Common Entrance results. Results are part of how a school markets itself.

Stellara is therefore sold to schools first (business to school to parent). Parents and children are the daily users; the school is the buyer and the channel.

5. MVP scope (Phase 1)

In

  1. School onboarding. Stellara staff create a school, the school admin invites teachers, and teachers create classes.
  2. Parent-led child profiles. Parents join with a class invite code, give consent, and create the child's profile.
  3. Child sign-in without credentials. On a family phone: a profile picker with a 4-picture PIN. On a school device: a class code plus the picture PIN.
  4. Placement check. About 20 items per paper, optional and resumable. Results are reported at strand level only.
  5. Learning path. Short lesson cards (5–8 minutes) followed by practice items on each topic.
  6. Practice and review. Scored practice, spaced review at +3, +10 and +30 days (capped by the exam date), and topic assessments.
  7. Practice tests. Timed, NCEE-style tests on a server-controlled timer. They can be resumed after an interruption.
  8. Mastery. Mastery rule v0, five levels per topic, shown as constellation stars. See 02 Learning rules.
  9. Motivation. Stardust (points with a daily cap), days in a row with resting clouds, and calm celebrations.
  10. Offline practice. Downloadable practice packs, answers saved on the device first, automatic sync.
  11. Parent report. A weekly in-app report plus a monthly WhatsApp or SMS summary, including "a note from Auntie Stella".
  12. Teacher dashboard. Class view by topic and mastery state, pupils who need help, and the ability to assign a practice set.
  13. School overview. Licence usage and Common Entrance coverage by class.
  14. Content studio. Author, review and publish items and lessons, with provenance and versioning.
  15. Compliance foundations. Consent records, a data export or delete request flow, an audit log and a non-affiliation notice.

6. Principles that shape every feature

  1. Learning is measured by rules, not opinions. Every status shown to a parent comes from a published rule.
  2. Nothing is earned by opening a page. Stardust and mastery come from answering, not viewing.
  3. No loss-framing. No broken-streak guilt, no countdowns other than real exam timers, no "hurry".
  4. Honest about the exam. Stellara shows practice coverage and mastery, never a predicted score and never "you are ready".
  5. Light on data and kind to cheap phones. See the budgets in 09 Frontend guidelines.
  6. Children's data is minimal. Collect a first name, a class, a year of birth and progress. No photos of children, and no free-text input from children in the MVP.

7. User stories and acceptance criteria

The IDs are used in tests (10 QA and testing).

7.1 Schools and teachers

SCH-01 As a Stellara operator, I can create a school with a licence count and term dates.

  • AC1: The school cannot invite teachers until a named school admin has accepted the data processing agreement.
  • AC2: Licence count and dates are visible to the school admin.

SCH-02 As a school admin, I can invite teachers by phone number or email.

  • AC1: An invite expires after 7 days.
  • AC2: A teacher sees only the classes assigned to them (enforced by row-level security, tested in pgTAP).

SCH-03 As a teacher, I can create a class (for example "Primary 5 Gold") and get a class invite code for parents.

  • AC1: The code is 6 characters, contains no ambiguous characters, and can be regenerated.
  • AC2: A child joins a class only after a parent has given consent (see FAM-02).

SCH-04 As a teacher, I can see a class grid of topics by pupils with mastery states.

  • AC1: States use shape as well as colour (outline, half, full, ringed).
  • AC2: A "needs help" list shows pupils with 3 or more topics at Foundation after at least 20 scored responses.

SCH-05 As a teacher, I can assign a practice set (topic or mixed) to a class with a due date.

  • AC1: Assigned sets appear first on the child's home screen.
  • AC2: Completion and the median score are shown to the teacher.

7.2 Families

FAM-01 As a parent, I can sign up with a phone number (OTP by WhatsApp, then SMS) or with email.

  • AC1: An OTP expires after 10 minutes. After 5 failed attempts in 15 minutes, sign-in is blocked for 30 minutes.

FAM-02 As a parent, I give consent before a child profile exists.

  • AC1: No child profile row exists without a consent record (database constraint plus test).
  • AC2: The consent record stores who consented, which child, the notice version, purposes, a timestamp and the verification method.
  • AC3: Each purpose is separately selectable: core learning (required), school sharing (required for school accounts), progress messages, product research.

FAM-03 As a parent, I create a child profile with a first name, year of birth, class code and an avatar chosen from a set.

  • AC1: Photo upload is not available.
  • AC2: The child chooses a 4-picture PIN on first use, and the parent can reset it.

FAM-04 As a parent, I receive a weekly report.

  • AC1: Every sentence is generated from rules in 02 Learning rules §8.
  • AC2: The report never shows a predicted score or the phrase "ready for".

FAM-05 As a parent, I can export or delete my child's data.

  • AC1: Export is delivered within 7 days as JSON plus a readable PDF.
  • AC2: Deletion removes the child's personal data within 30 days and keeps only anonymised aggregate counts.

FAM-06 As a parent, I can withdraw consent as easily as I gave it.

  • AC1: Withdrawal takes 2 taps from Settings. Processing stops immediately, and deletion follows FAM-05.

7.3 Children

KID-01 As a child, I pick my profile and enter my picture PIN.

  • AC1: After 5 wrong PINs, the profile is locked for 5 minutes and a parent can unlock it.

KID-02 As a child, I see my sky: today's suggested set, assigned sets, and my constellation.

  • AC1: The first screen is usable within 5 seconds on the reference device (see 09).

KID-03 As a child, I take a short lesson and then practise.

  • AC1: Practice items are scored on the server. On a correct first attempt, the child sees feedback within 100 ms locally (optimistic), and the server confirms it.
  • AC2: A wrong answer shows one hint and then the worked explanation. It never says "wrong"; it says "Not quite".

KID-04 As a Common Entrance candidate, I take a timed practice test.

  • AC1: The server owns the start time and the end time. A lost connection does not stop the clock, and answers saved locally are accepted up to 15 minutes after the end time if they were recorded before it.
  • AC2: A test can be resumed twice, with a total pause time of up to 20 minutes. After that it is scored as it stands.
  • AC3: Answer keys are never sent to the device for a practice test.

KID-05 As a child, I can practise offline with a downloaded pack.

  • AC1: A pack of 40 items with images is under 120 KB.
  • AC2: Offline responses sync automatically when online. They count towards days in a row if synced within 72 hours.

KID-06 As a child, I get Stardust and keep my days in a row.

  • AC1: The rules are as in 02 §6–7.
  • AC2: A missed day uses a resting cloud automatically. The copy never mentions losing anything.

7.4 Content team

CON-01 As an author, I create items in the schema in 03.

  • AC1: An item cannot be published without provenance, a verified answer, a reviewer other than the author, alt text for every image, and a curriculum tag.

CON-02 As a reviewer, I approve, reject or request changes, with comments.

  • AC1: Every state change is in the audit log.

CON-03 As an author, I edit a published item by creating a new version.

  • AC1: Responses keep the item version they were answered against.

02 · Learning rules

These are the rules the product runs on. The implemented baseline is v0.1.0, with the clarifications approved on 13 November 2026 below. Thresholds remain subject to pilot tuning. Rule changes need a version bump (rules_version) and new fixtures. Technical acceptance does not replace product sign-off.

Implementation: rules live in one pure TypeScript package, packages/rules, used by Edge Functions and tests. The UI never computes a scored outcome on its own. See 04.

1. Definitions

Term Meaning
Item One question (version-pinned). Has a difficulty: easy, standard or hard. May be tagged application.
Response One answer to one item, stored in attempts (immutable).
Scored response A response that counts as evidence of learning (§2).
Set A group of items done in one sitting: practice set, review set, topic assessment, placement check or practice test.
Topic The smallest curriculum node with its own mastery state (for example "Equivalent fractions").
Strand A group of topics (for example "Fractions, decimals and percentages").
Learning day A calendar day that ends at 03:00 local time (default Africa/Lagos).

2. Scored response

A response is scored when all of these are true:

  1. It is the first attempt on this item by this child, or the child has not seen the item for 30 days.
  2. It was given before any hint or explanation for this item was shown in this sitting.
  3. Response time is at least min_response_ms (default 2,500 ms; 1,500 ms for items flagged quick).
  4. It was made in a set whose answer key was not on the device, or it is an offline response with key_on_device = true, in which case it is scored with confidence = 'low' (see §3.4).
  5. The child profile was in normal mode (not "practice with a grown-up" mode).

Everything else is stored as practice and never moves mastery.

3. Mastery (rule v0.1.0)

3.1 Levels

Level Code Rule at topic level
— not_assessed Fewer than 4 scored responses
1 foundation At least 4 scored responses; Level 2 not met
2 developing At least 6 scored responses; at least 5 of the last 10 correct
3 proficient At least 10 scored; 8 of the last 10 correct; 6 of those 8 are standard or hard; across at least 2 sets on 2 different learning days. Or a Topic Assessment passed at 8/10 or better
4 advanced Level 3, plus 4 of the last 5 hard items correct across 2 sets, including at least 2 application items
5 mastered Level 4, plus a retention check passed at 4/5 on unseen mixed items at least 14 days after reaching Level 4 (7 days if the child's exam is under 6 weeks away)

3.2 Moving down

  • A retention check scoring 2/5 or lower drops the topic one level.
  • No other event lowers a level. A bad practice set alone never does.

3.3 Caps

  • Placement returns strand results and starting-topic recommendations only; it never changes topic mastery, including when all answers are correct.
  • Level 3 and above use only confidence = 'normal' responses for every count, accuracy window, assessment and retention requirement.
  • Returning to Level 4 after a retention downgrade starts a fresh 14-day wait (7 days when the exam is strictly under 42 days away).

3.4 Offline evidence

Offline practice packs may carry answer keys so children get instant feedback. Those responses are scored with confidence = 'low'. They count towards Levels 1–2 only. Practice tests never ship keys.

4. Spaced review

  • After a topic reaches Level 2, schedule reviews at +3, +10 and +30 days.
  • A review is 3–5 items from the topic, mixed into the next review set.
  • Anchor the offsets at first attainment of Level 2. If necessary, proportionally scale all three offsets to the exam-minus-3-day cutoff, round down to whole calendar days, omit nonfuture dates and merge duplicates. Return no_future_slot when no future date fits.
  • A missed review stays due and does not pile up. At most 3 due topics are pulled into one review set.

5.2 Timer and interruption rules

  • The server sets started_at and ends_at when a session starts.
  • The client shows ends_at - server_now, synced each minute, and counts down locally.
  • Answers are saved locally first, then sent in batches every 10 answers or every 30 s.
  • If the connection drops, the child keeps going. Locally saved answers with answered_at <= ends_at are accepted up to 15 minutes after ends_at.
  • Resume: at most 2 resumes, with a total pause of up to 20 minutes (the app closed or the device switched off). The timer does not stop for a lost connection, only for an app close counted as a pause.
  • A validated pause extends ends_at by its actual duration. The future server adapter must validate pause evidence; an arbitrary client claim is insufficient. Answers during a pause are rejected. A third resume or excess total pause finalizes the session. The server accounts for an ongoing validated pause, bounded by the remaining pause allowance, before applying the grace period.
  • At ends_at plus the grace period, the server finalises the session and scores what it has.
  • A response time below 1,000 ms on more than 30% of items flags the session as suspect_timing (shown to the teacher, not the child).

6. Stardust

Stardust is a motivation currency. It never enters mastery and is never sold.

Event Stardust
Correct scored response +10
Correct response on a due review item +5 extra
Completed review set +20
Completed practice test with at least 80% of items answered +30
Topic reaches a new level +25
  • Daily cap: 200 Stardust per learning day. Above the cap, events are logged with 0 value.
  • Awards are clamped to the remaining allowance: 195 plus a +10 event awards 5. A topic's new highest-level transition awards +25 once; a jump from Level 0 to Level 3 awards +25, and regaining an old level awards nothing. Correct scored placement answers earn ordinary Stardust.
  • All awards go to stardust_ledger (append-only), written only by the server.
  • No Stardust for opening lessons, watching or logging in.

Level names (by total Stardust): Spark 0 · Glow 500 · Beam 1,500 · Comet 3,500 · Nebula 7,000 · Galaxy 12,000. These describe light, not attainment.

The weekly report is generated on Sunday at 18:00 Lagos time using consecutive Sunday-18:00 cutoffs, with an inclusive start and exclusive end. Late-Sunday activity belongs to the next report. Display the actual period; do not describe this snapshot as a complete Monday-to-Monday learning week.

9. Placement check

  • 20 items per configured learning area, spread evenly across strands. Optional and resumable. Learning areas are distinct from official exam papers.
  • Result per strand: "Looks secure so far" (≥ 75% correct and at least 4 items), "Check this" (< 50%), otherwise "Not yet measured enough".
  • It never reports topic-level results or changes topic mastery, and is never called a test.

A shared passage, table or diagram used by 2–10 items (for example one comprehension passage with 5 questions). The fields are id, version, kind, body, media, alt_text, provenance and licence_ref.

4.4 Maths and media

  • No video in the MVP.

  • Online practice: the UI waits for the server result (target p95 under 400 ms in Lagos). It shows the answer as "checking" with no correctness hint until then.

6. Identity and sessions

  • Adults use Supabase Auth: phone OTP (WhatsApp first, then SMS), or an email magic link. Staff roles require MFA (TOTP).
  • Children have no Supabase Auth user. A child acts through a child session:
    • Family device: the parent is signed in. The child picks their profile and enters a 4-picture PIN. The Edge Function child-session returns a short-lived JWT (2 h, refreshable on the device for 30 days) with claims { role: 'child', child_id, guardian_id }.
    • School device: the teacher starts "class mode" with a class code. The child picks their name and enters their picture PIN. The JWT has { role: 'child', child_id, class_id, school_id, mode: 'class' } and expires at the end of the school day.
  • PINs are stored as salted hashes (argon2id) in child_pins. Five wrong attempts lock the profile for 5 minutes.
  • Shared phones: each child profile has its own IndexedDB partition, keyed by child_id.

6. Security controls

Target: OWASP ASVS Level 2 for the web app and functions.

  • Data isolation: RLS on every table, with tests for every policy (10).
  • Secrets: Supabase service key only in Edge Functions; no secrets in the client; secrets rotated quarterly.
  • Auth: OTP rate limits; MFA for staff; child PINs hashed with argon2id; child JWTs short-lived.
  • Answer keys: never in online set or test payloads; offline packs assumed extractable, so their items are kept out of test pools.
  • Integrity: append-only attempts, stardust_ledger, audit_log; idempotent submissions; suspicious timing flagged.
  • Transport and storage: TLS 1.2+; encryption at rest (provider); signed URLs for exports, expiring in 24 h.
  • Dependencies: Renovate, pnpm audit in CI, lockfile required.
  • Headers: strict CSP, no third-party scripts on logged-in surfaces, frame-ancestors 'none'.
  • Logging: no personal data in logs; Sentry PII scrubbing; request IDs.
  • Pen test: an external test before the public launch.

7. Breach runbook (outline)

  1. Detect and record (time, source, scope).
  2. Contain (rotate keys, disable accounts, block routes).
  3. Assess the risk to children and parents within 24 hours.
  4. Notify the NDPC within 72 hours where required; notify parents and schools in plain language.
  5. Fix, review, and update the DPIA and controls.

8. Claims, marketing and exam bodies

  • No guaranteed results, predicted scores or "you are ready" in product or marketing.

  • Never use "expo", "runz" or "live questions".
  • Keep a claims register: every marketing claim with dated evidence. Check ARCON pre-vetting requirements before paid advertising.

From UN General Comment 25 and the brand's own rules:

  • No dark patterns: no loss-framed streaks, no countdown pressure beyond real exam timers, no purchases inside children's screens.
  • Notifications at most once a day, never during quiet hours (20:00–07:00) or school hours by default.
  • No social features, chat or public profiles for children in the MVP.
  • No AI features for children until a DPIA update and DPO sign-off.

08 · Design system

The visual direction is Sweet garden: strawberry, honey and matcha on cream, with warm cocoa text. It feels sweet, calm and a little cartoony, in both light and dark. The idea behind it is simple: learning grows. Every topic is a star, and a mastered star sprouts. The full visual kit is published as the Stellara Brand Kit, with a light/dark switch on every board. This document is the build reference: tokens, components and rules. Tokens live in packages/ui/tokens; the source of truth for colour values is docs/tools/tokens.mjs.

1. Principles

  1. Sweet, then calm. Sweetness lives in colour, round shapes and the logo. Layout, motion and words stay quiet. Nothing flashes, bounces or shakes.
  2. One thing at a time. One question per screen, one Strawberry button, plenty of space.
  3. Shape before colour. Every state can be read without colour (icons, words, shapes).
  4. Earned, not given. Honey (rewards) appears only after real learning.
  5. Both themes, always. Every screen, the children's app included, is designed and tested in Daytime and Starlight.
  6. Kind to cheap phones. Light assets, no heavy effects inside lessons.

2.3 Daytime and Starlight (light and dark)

In every user-facing word, the light theme is Daytime and the dark theme is Starlight; following the device is Match my phone (see 16 §4). "Light" and "dark" are used only in code.

  • Default: follow the device (prefers-color-scheme). This covers the children's app, the parent app, teacher and school screens, and the website.
  • Override: parents can choose Settings → How Stellara looks: Match my phone, Daytime, Starlight. It is stored per device (localStorage stellara-theme, absent means System), so a shared family tablet keeps its own setting. There is no theme switch on children's screens. This follows Apple's guidance (HIG Dark Mode: "Avoid offering an app-specific appearance setting"), while still giving families the control they asked for.
  • Dark is warm cocoa, never pure black or purple. Text is a soft off-white (#F7ECE7), never #FFFFFF on dark.
  • Illustrations keep their colours in dark mode. Auntie Stella and photos are dimmed slightly (filter: brightness(.9)) so they don't glare.
  • The logo keeps the same honey star and sprout in both themes. Only the wordmark colour changes (primary-ink).
  • Switching theme changes colours instantly. Nothing animates.

8. Themes (light and dark)

  • Tokens only. Components read colour from CSS variables (var(--primary), Tailwind bg-primary), never hex values. A lint rule (stylelint-declaration-strict-value for colour properties, plus a grep in CI for #[0-9a-f]{6} in apps/*/src) blocks raw colours outside packages/ui/tokens.
  • Resolution order: data-theme on <html> (set from the parent's per-device choice) wins; otherwise prefers-color-scheme. Absence of the stored key means System.
  • No flash on load. A tiny inline script in <head>, before any CSS paints, reads localStorage["stellara-theme"] inside try/catch and sets data-theme. Also set <meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark"> and <meta name="theme-color"> for both media queries so the browser UI matches.
  • Switching is instant. Changing theme swaps variables only; nothing transitions. Canvas and 3D scenes re-read the variables on a matchMedia("(prefers-color-scheme: dark)") change and on data-theme change.
  • Images. SVG icons use currentColor. Illustrations keep their colours; Auntie Stella and photos get filter: brightness(.9) in dark. Never ship a white-background PNG.
  • PWA. The manifest's background_color is cream; the splash in dark comes from theme-color with media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)".
  • Contrast in CI. node docs/tools/tokens.mjs runs on every pull request and fails if any text pair drops below 4.5:1 (3:1 for the focus ring) in either theme.

9. Motion in code

  • Use the tokens --ease-out, --ease-in-out, --motion-press, --motion-ui, --motion-reward (08 §5).
  • Press feedback: transition: transform var(--motion-press) var(--ease-out) and :active { transform: scale(.97) }.
  • Only transform and opacity animate. Never transition: all.
  • Prefer CSS transitions (interruptible) over keyframes for UI that can be re-triggered. Enter with @starting-style where supported, a data-mounted attribute otherwise.
  • Hover styles go inside @media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine).
  • prefers-reduced-motion: reduce keeps opacity fades and removes movement, scaling and 3D.

16 · UX writing

Every word in Stellara is part of the design. This guide covers the app, notifications, WhatsApp, SMS and email. The look is sweet; the words are sweet in moments and plain in facts. A child should feel encouraged, and a parent should know exactly what happened and what to do next.

Strings live in packages/i18n (ICU MessageFormat). Email templates are in docs/emails/ and are built by docs/tools/emails.mjs. The voice rules in 08 §10 are summarised here and extended.

1. Principles

  1. Kind and true. We never shame, nag or exaggerate. We never hide a problem either: "Not quite" is kind and honest.
  2. Specific beats general. "9 of the last 10 right" beats "Great progress!". Praise names what went right.
  3. One idea per line. Children read one short idea at a time. Parents get the fact first and the detail second.
  4. Same thing, same name. A feature keeps one name everywhere: app, WhatsApp, email, school letters. A button called "Start today's set" leads to a screen titled "Today's set".
  5. Sweet in moments, plain in facts. Garden and sky words belong to celebrations, Auntie Stella, and the names of things. Settings, consent, money, errors and data use plain words.
  6. Nothing to lose. Progress only grows. We never say lose, break, miss, fail, behind or last chance.

2. Three voices

Voice Speaks to Sounds like Rules
Auntie Stella Children A patient auntie at the kitchen table Ages 4–7: ≤ 8 words a line, read aloud. Ages 8–12: ≤ 15 words. One exclamation mark per screen at most. "My dear" no more than once a session
Stellara Parents and guardians A trusted class teacher writing a note home Evidence first. Full sentences. No exclamation marks, no emoji in the app. Uses the child's first name, never "your child" when the name is known

10. Writing for Nigerian families

  • Write for a reading age of about 9 in child copy and 12 in parent copy. Short words, short sentences.
  • Avoid idioms that don't travel ("knock it out of the park"). Nigerian English is the default; it is not American English.
  • Leave 30% extra space for translation. Yoruba, Hausa and Igbo versions come after the MVP; until then, Auntie Stella's lines may include a short greeting in the child's home language chosen by the parent ("Ẹ káàbọ̀, Tolu").
  • Read-aloud: every child line must sound right spoken. Test lines out loud before they ship.

17 · Foundations and human gates

1. Engineering deliverables

The monorepo uses Node 22, pnpm and Turborepo. Next.js 16 hosts the minimal web shell; packages provide pure learning rules, client-safe and server-only Zod schemas, theme tokens and English-first catalogues. Supabase directories reserve future work only: no database or cloud resources are created by this foundation.

The pure rules package receives explicit state, identifiers, timestamps and timezone. It returns immutable decisions and audit events. Server adapters must validate identity, clock and pause evidence, deduplicate durable submissions, and persist results transactionally. Passing library tests does not prove those future database and API integrations work.

2. Approved v0.1.0 clarifications

The learning specification records the detailed thresholds. The approved choices are: normal-confidence evidence only for Levels 3–5; strand-only placement with ordinary scored-answer Stardust; proportional exam review compression; validated pauses extending test deadlines; first-highest-transition milestone rewards; partial awards at the daily cap; voluntary Rest Mode activity counting; a fresh retention waiting period after returning to Level 4; and repair counting actual activity only.

Reports use consecutive Sunday 18:00 Lagos cutoffs. Late-Sunday activity belongs to the next report; the report must show its actual period.

3. Academic review

The curriculum draft contains 74 topics across five learning areas, not five official exam papers. It is an original planning draft awaiting academic approval. Exam papers are modelled separately. The 2027 mapping, official item counts and durations are not filled with guesses.

Reference: NECO's 2026 general information describes two grouped papers. It does not confirm the 2027 format. Next.js 16 was selected because Next.js 15 security support ends on 31 January 2027, before the pilot finishes.

Academic review must confirm topic coverage, age suitability, prerequisite edges, religious/moral education options, vocabulary, item allocation and exam mapping. An unverified blueprint cannot pass the published-blueprint schema.
